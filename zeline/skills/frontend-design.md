# Frontend Design

> Art-directed frontend: strong landing pages, apps, prototypes, and game UIs with restrained composition, image-led hierarchy, and tasteful motion.

Use this skill when the task asks for a visually strong landing page, website, app, prototype, demo, or game UI — and the quality of the work depends on art direction, hierarchy, restraint, imagery, and motion rather than component count.

Goal: ship interfaces that feel deliberate, premium, and current. Default toward award-level composition: one big idea, strong imagery, sparse copy, rigorous spacing, and a small number of memorable motions.

## Working Model

Before building, write three things:

- **Visual thesis:** one sentence describing mood, material, and energy
- **Content plan:** hero, support, detail, final CTA
- **Interaction thesis:** 2-3 motion ideas that change the feel of the page

Each section gets one job, one dominant visual idea, and one primary takeaway or action.

## Beautiful Defaults

- Start with composition, not components.
- Prefer a full-bleed hero or full-canvas visual anchor.
- Use large, confident type. One expressive display face for headlines, one clean face for body. Avoid more than two families.
- Generous whitespace. Let the layout breathe; crowding reads as cheap.
- Limit the palette to 2-3 colors plus neutrals. Derive accents from the imagery, not from a random swatch.
- Imagery leads. A single strong photo, render, or illustration beats a grid of stock icons.
- Motion is seasoning, not the meal: a few deliberate transitions (reveal on scroll, parallax hero, hover state) rather than everything animating.

## Anti-Patterns To Avoid

- Generic card grids with identical rounded rectangles.
- Weak or absent branding — no clear visual identity.
- Icon soup: rows of tiny meaningless icons.
- Walls of copy. Cut text to the minimum that still communicates.
- Random gradients and drop shadows applied everywhere.
- Motion clutter: everything sliding, bouncing, or fading at once.

## Composition Checklist

1. Does one big idea dominate the first screen?
2. Is there a clear visual hierarchy (what to read first, second, third)?
3. Is the type scale confident and consistent?
4. Is spacing rigorous and rhythmic (a consistent spacing scale)?
5. Does imagery carry the design rather than decorate it?
6. Are there 2-3 memorable motions, and nothing more?
7. Would this look intentional next to award-winning sites?

## Delivery

- Match the host project's framework, tokens, and conventions.
- Reuse existing components and design tokens instead of introducing new ones.
- Validate the final result visually before calling it done.
